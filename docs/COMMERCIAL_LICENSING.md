# Chionia Lab commercial licensing

This is a draft plan for licensing Chionia Lab's project-authored materials.
Confirm ownership and contributor authority before publishing it as an active
license offer. The materials are intended to remain proprietary. Under the
proposed terms, non-commercial evaluation is allowed; a business, paid product,
client engagement, monetized service, or other Commercial Use needs a separate
written license with the applicable rightsholder(s), with the agreed fee paid
on the terms in that agreement.

## How a commercial request should work

1. Identify the exact code, lesson content, visual assets, and other materials
   the prospective licensee wants to use.
2. Confirm who owns or controls each item and whether any contributor or third
   party has rights in it.
3. Agree in writing on the licensee, permitted product and purpose, territory,
   duration, distribution, support obligations, and price (for example, a
   fixed fee, annual fee, per-seat fee, or royalty).
4. Have the applicable rightsholder(s) and licensee sign the agreement.
5. Collect the agreed fee as specified in the agreement before commercial use
   starts, then retain the signed agreement and payment record.

The repository owner can receive initial inquiries through GitHub. Before
actively offering licenses, set up a durable business contact and payment
process. Do not promise a price, grant permission, or invoice under a company
name until the legal rightsholder and any required business entity are
confirmed.

## Ownership and release checklist

- Confirm the author identities and rights in the Git history. The public
  profile for the repository owner `s3969084` identifies as Angelos Christou,
  matching the other commit author name. Verify that both author identities
  are yours and that no employment, school, or third-party agreement affects
  the work before claiming exclusive control or licensing it.
- Review contributions, lesson text, generated data, images, fonts, and other
  assets for third-party or contributor rights. Preserve their separate
  notices and comply with their licenses.
- Choose the legal person or entity that will sign and receive payment. A
  project or brand name alone may not identify the party able to enforce the
  agreement.
- Decide commercial pricing and standard deal terms. The repository license
  intentionally contains no automatic price: payment obligations should be
  stated in the separate agreement accepted by the licensee.
- Keep dated source archives, releases, contributor records, signed agreements,
  invoices, and payment records. Copyright is automatic for software in many
  countries, but ownership evidence and records are still useful in disputes
  and licensing.
- Review the license and commercial agreement with an IP lawyer in the
  applicable jurisdiction before relying on them for enforcement.

## Public GitHub repository note

The repository is currently public on GitHub. GitHub's current Terms of Service
allow other users to view and fork public repositories, grant users certain
rights to use repository content through GitHub's functionality, and say the
terms generally do not restrict lawful access to or use of public repository
contents. Those terms may affect how the proposed restrictions apply on the
platform. Adding a restrictive license does not recall copies made outside
GitHub, settle ownership, or guarantee payment from someone who uses material
without permission. Review the current GitHub terms and repository visibility
with an IP lawyer before relying on this policy. Do not assume a new license
overrides platform terms or rights relating to existing forks.

## What this policy does and does not do

The policy makes the intended offer explicit: limited non-commercial
evaluation is allowed; commercial use needs a separate paid agreement first.
It does not create a fee automatically when someone uses the project, prove
that one person owns every file, or guarantee that a court will award payment.
If unauthorized commercial use is found, preserve evidence and get legal advice
before sending a demand or filing a platform complaint.

Because the proposed license restricts commercial use, Chionia Lab would be
source-available under this policy, not open-source under the Open Source
Definition. Third-party dependencies retain their own licenses.
